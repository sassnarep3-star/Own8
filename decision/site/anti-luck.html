Anti-luck rules

How to keep luck out of the leaderboard — and what the current design gets wrong.

Why this page exists

Even under the most favourable read (n=15, one week), the benchmarks tie a naive rule and lose to a simpler one. That is the single most decision-relevant result available today.

These are defects in the project's own design, not in the NFL's data. Each is fixable without any new data.

What "removing luck" can honestly mean

Luck cannot be deleted from a result. What can be done is to make luck's expected contribution small and visible:

  • Compress by sample, not by confidence. Require a minimum number of slates before a score counts.
  • Compare, never report alone. Every number sits beside a fixed naive rule scored on the same games.
  • Count the attempts. Register every variant up front and adjust for the whole family, because a p<0.01 will appear by chance when enough variants are tried.
  • Separate error from outcome. A correct pick and a well-estimated line are different achievements; report both.
  • Reward the process, not the run. Pay for beating a baseline over a long sample with a stated uncertainty interval, never for a hot week.
Applied to today's evidence

On Week 9 the benchmark ties the naive point-differential rule exactly and loses to the naive win-percentage rule. Under any rule in the list above, that scores zero. Saying so is the useful result.
