Executive summary

The short version: what is verified, what the first real test showed, and what needs a decision.

Bottom line in seven lines

  1. The site is honest about what it does not know, and that is its strength. Every blocked gate, every blank cell and every flag is correctly labelled. Nothing found this week contradicts that discipline.
  2. The published dashboard is stale. It was frozen at 2026-11-04, before Week 9 was played. All 15 Week 9 games are now final and Week 10 is under way.
  3. The first real out-of-sample test is in, and it does not show skill. The frozen lines picked 13 of 15 winners (86.7%); a naive point-differential rule scored 86.7% on the same games and a naive win-percentage rule scored 92.3%.
  4. A spectacular-looking p-value is a trap here. The coin-flip p-value is 0.007, which would look like proof of skill; the baseline comparison shows it is not.
  5. Starting-lineup depth cannot be tracked from official data. nfl.com publishes no depth chart and no snap counts; only position, roster status and injury designation are official. The requested 2nd/3rd/4th-string fields must stay blank.
  6. No strategy can ever be rewarded under the current rules, because three of the eight gates require prices that do not exist in any official source. That is a design problem, not a data problem.
  7. Six decisions are waiting for a human, and none of them needs new data. They are on the Suggestions page.
