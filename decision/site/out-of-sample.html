Week 9: the first real test

The site published 15 explicitly unvalidated Week 9 lines. Those games are now final. This is what happened.

Result

Benchmark favourite hit 13 of 15 (86.7%). Naive point-differential rule: 86.7% on the same games. Naive win-percentage rule: 92.3%. The benchmark does not beat the simplest rule available.

Signed margin bias

B0 applies NO venue adjustment by design. Across Week 9 it projected a mean home margin of -1.367 against a realised -4.6; the positive signed bias means it under-weighted the home side, consistent with a missing home-field term. One week cannot fix the size of that term and no value is assumed here.

What this does and does not prove

  • One week of 15 games cannot distinguish skill from luck; the honest band on a 15-game hit rate is enormous.
  • No operator price existed for these games, so no ROI, no edge and no reward can be computed. Moneyline/return claims remain blocked.
  • The only defensible skill test is the benchmark's error versus a fixed naive rule on identical games. On Week 9 that gap is zero against point differential and negative against win percentage.
  • Six of fifteen games missed the margin by more than two touchdowns, which is where a real model difference would have to show up.
Read this before quoting the number

The 13/15 hit rate is NOT evidence of skill: a rule that simply picks the team with the better season point differential is scored on the same 15 games in the same output. The B0 edge over that rule, if any, is the only part that could be skill; with n=15 the confidence interval on any difference spans zero. Nothing here clears a validation gate.
