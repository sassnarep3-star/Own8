Strategy board

Twelve strategies, three of them scoreable, ranked by evidence rather than by profit.

The ranking rule, stated before any week is scored

Ranking is by the lower 95% bootstrap bound of per-bet skill, defined as the model's probability minus the de-vigged market probability on the side the strategy chose. ROI is shown in the next column and is never used to set a rank. That single choice is why a +21.0% ROI sits at rank 2 of 3 rather than rank 1.

The headline: identical picks, identical profit, opposite skill

Three moneyline strategies, one set of picks

S6 / S7 / S8 · 15 games · Week 9 official prices

  • All three chose the identical side in all 15 games. They went 13–2 and returned +21.0% each.
  • Per-bet skill: S6 -0.0928, S7 -0.1120, S8 +0.0282.
  • Holm-adjusted significance on skill: S6 and S7 are significantly negative (p = 0.002). S8 is not significant (p = 0.922).
  • Holm-adjusted significance on ROI across the scored family: p = 0.74. Nothing here is distinguishable from luck.
  • Ranking by ROI would have promoted S8 over S6 and S7 for no reason other than the price on the winners. Ranking by lower-bound skill puts S7 last, which is correct: it was the worst-calibrated of the three.

A caveat on the multiple-comparison correction

The Holm correction is applied across the three scored strategies only. Excluding the nine unpriceable ones makes the correction less conservative than a family that included them, which is the correct direction to err in when the alternative is a false claim of skill.
