Q-01 · Can our own line beat the market's line on the same games?

NO owner: project owner, after 4-6 more weeks of capture

Answerable from today's information: NO - not from information that exists today. The direction is unfavourable and the interval is wide.

Why it blocks a decision. Every reward, ranking and stake rule depends on this. If our number is worse than the market's, a leaderboard that rewards 'line accuracy' rewards noise.

  • What we have. Week 9, the only week with both an official price and an official result: our spread MAE 10.65 vs the market's 8.6667; our number was closer in 4 of 15 games; mean paired difference +1.9834 pts with a 95% interval of [0.6684, 3.2949]; moneyline Brier 0.189867 vs 0.118439 de-vigged.
  • What we lack. A sample large enough to separate a small edge from zero. Fifteen games cannot settle this either way, and Week 10 had not been played at capture time.
  • How to discover it. Score 60+ priced games. That means collecting the weekly picks article every week from here, which is a repeatable 15-minute job, and grading only after finals. No new data source is needed.
  • Blocks. the entire leaderboard reward rule, and any decision to promote a live strategy
  • Reference. official reference ↗

Why it blocks a decision. Six suggestion registers and four leaderboards rank on four different quantities (loss, LCB of loss reduction, skill, ROI). A reward attached to the wrong one pays luck.

Q-04 · Is the injury-and-replacement signal obtainable before kickoff?

NO owner: project owner decides whether to run the 6-8 week collection

Answerable from today's information: NO for pregame depth order. YES for named quarterback changes and postgame starter facts.

Why it blocks a decision. The operator asked for 2nd/3rd/4th-string tracking. A model that ignores a quarterback change is measurably worse; one that invents depth is worse still.

  • What we have. 28 clubs, 345 official injury rows, and 6 replacement cases confirmed with official URLs. The depth-chart URL returns the official 404; the roster publishes a transaction status, not an order; the Game Book PDF names the 11 starters but only after the game.
  • What we lack. Any pregame source of depth order. There is none on nfl.com, at any URL tested today.
  • How to discover it. Collect the postgame Game Book every week and join it to the pregame injury report. After 6-8 weeks you have a measured 'backup started' event set. That is the only honest route.
  • Blocks. any injury adjustment inside the line model
  • Reference. official reference ↗

Q-05 · Which correlation numbers may be used inside a parlay price, and which may not?

PARTLY owner: project owner + modelling owner

Answerable from today's information: PARTLY - the surviving values are usable as raw associations; none of them is a priced joint distribution.

Why it blocks a decision. A same-game parlay is priced from a joint probability. Using the wrong correlation silently misprices every combo.

  • What we have. 7 values, each with n on the row. Three survive Holm: QB yards vs own points r=0.3852 (n=146), QB yards vs game total r=0.3488 (n=146), opposing QBs r=0.5418 (n=67). Side vs total on realised outcomes is r=-0.1608 (n=136) and does NOT survive correction.
  • What we lack. Any correlation measured on the residual of a forecast rather than on raw outcomes, and any dependence estimate that survives out of sample.
  • How to discover it. Model the joint distribution directly on residual pairs, walk-forward, and score the resulting joint probability against the realised joint outcome. Needs no new data.
  • Blocks. pricing for parlays, same-game parlays and prop combos
  • Reference. official reference ↗

Q-07 · How many bets make a result interpretable?

YES owner: nobody - it is arithmetic

Answerable from today's information: YES - the arithmetic is settled. 15 cannot do it; the site now refuses to call anything an edge below 30 settled bets.

Why it blocks a decision. A 13-of-15 moneyline run looks like 86.7% skill and is entirely consistent with a coin flip at these prices.

  • What we have. At the Week 9 prices the break-even rate is 0.582; 13 of 15 wins gives a Wilson 95% interval of [0.566, 0.823] and a two-sided p of 0.104 against break-even.
  • What we lack. A sample size large enough to separate a 3% edge from zero. With ~150 games per week-priced slate, that is a season, not a week.
  • How to discover it. Nothing to discover. The rule is implemented; the wait is calendar time.
  • Blocks. any claim of a profitable strategy before a season of data
  • Reference. official reference ↗

Q-10 · Is the Week 10 line set even comparable to the market?

PARTLY owner: modelling owner

Answerable from today's information: PARTLY - comparable in convention, not yet comparable in calibration.

Why it blocks a decision. The difference between our handicap and the market's is the basis of every 'edge' column. If the two are on different scales, the column is meaningless.

  • What we have. Our Week 10 home handicap and the market's, on the same row, in one convention, for 13 games. The market-implied handicap is derived through our own sigma, and where it disagrees with the published handicap the gap is our model's sigma, not the market being wrong.
  • What we lack. An independent estimate of the game-margin standard deviation from realised results rather than from the model's own training residuals.
  • How to discover it. Estimate sigma from the realised margin distribution of the 136-game log and re-derive. ~10 lines of code, no new data.
  • Blocks. the 'difference' columns and any threshold set on them
  • Reference. official reference ↗
