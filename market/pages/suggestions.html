SG-01 · Name one ranking quantity and retire the other three

OPEN - FOR OPERATOR DECISION P1

Four leaderboards rank on four quantities. A reward attached to loss-reduction is paid to a forecaster; a reward attached to ROI is paid to a staking run. Neither is 'skill' without a correction.

ARank on the lower 95% bound of per-bet skill vs the de-vigged market price, after Holm. ROI is displayed and never ranked.
BRank on flat-stake ROI but require the lower bound of per-bet skill to exceed 0 as an entry gate.
CTwo boards: a Signal board (skill, ranked) and a Staking board (ROI, not ranked, no reward).
DLeave all four in place and let the reader decide.

Recommendation. C. It is the only option where a lucky staking run cannot mask a bad signal, and it is what the Week 9 data already shows: three moneyline strategies with identical +21.0% ROI and skill from -0.112 to +0.028.

  • Cost. 1 page of copy and a change to one ranking key. No new data.
  • Effect on the gates. Decides whether any strategy can ever be 'ranked'. No gate is relaxed.
  • What must be verified. Rank order must be reproducible byte-for-byte from market/data/tournament.json.
  • Reference. source ↗

SG-02 · Capture the price page twice a week to get a real opening and closing pair

OPEN - FOR OPERATOR DECISION P1

The site now has one price per game, locked Thursday 2 p.m. ET. It has no opening number and no closing number, so closing-line value - the standard test of whether a model adds anything - cannot be computed.

ACapture Tuesday and 90 minutes before kickoff, every week, and store both.
BCapture only the Thursday-locked number and never claim closing-line value.
CCapture every 6 hours and build a full price path.

Recommendation. A. Two fetches a week, and it unlocks the single most respected test available without extra data.

  • Cost. 2 fetches per week, ~5 minutes, plus a small append-only store.
  • Effect on the gates. Adds a real gate: a strategy must beat the closing number, not a mid-week number.
  • What must be verified. Re-read the same page twice and confirm the numbers actually moved before claiming a close.
  • Reference. source ↗

SG-06 · Approve the demotion thresholds so a live strategy can be sent back to paper

OPEN - FOR OPERATOR DECISION P2

Rules D1-D8 exist and are tested, but no threshold was ever approved, so the gate cannot fire.

ADemote when the lower 95% bound of per-bet skill is below 0 over the last 40 priced bets.
BDemote on a drawdown of 20 units.
CDemote when the strategy's Brier score is worse than the de-vigged market over 40 bets.
DNever demote.

Recommendation. A and C together. B is a staking rule and belongs on the staking board, not the signal board.

  • Cost. One config change; the evaluator already implements all of them.
  • Effect on the gates. Makes the live/paper distinction real rather than decorative.
  • What must be verified. Re-run the 23 existing unit tests after the thresholds change.
  • Reference. source ↗

SG-08 · Estimate sigma from realised results before publishing any more 'implied' numbers

OPEN - FOR OPERATOR DECISION P2

The market-implied handicap is produced by running a probability through our normal model. If sigma is wrong, the implied column is wrong and the difference column is wrong with it.

AEstimate sigma from the realised margin distribution of the 136-game log and re-derive, showing both.
BKeep the model-residual sigma and label the column 'model-implied'.
CDrop the implied column and publish only the market's own numbers.

Recommendation. A, with C as the fallback if the two sigmas disagree materially.

  • Cost. ~10 lines of code, no new data.
  • Effect on the gates. Improves the credibility of every difference column.
  • What must be verified. Both sigmas must be printed side by side.
  • Reference. source ↗

If you only act on five

The shortest defensible path to a competition that rewards skill rather than luck

  1. SG-05 first, because it decides whether the competition may exist at all. Paper points only.
  2. SG-01 second, because a reward needs a ranking quantity and there are currently four.
  3. SG-03 third, because the repository's central claim about prices is now wrong and everything downstream inherits the error.
  4. SG-02 and SG-04 in parallel, because they are the only two things that add real information rather than rearranging what exists.
  5. SG-06 and SG-07 last, because a demotion gate and a canonical hub are only meaningful once there is something to demote and somewhere to read it.

And explicitly not:

  • Do not promote any strategy to live before 60 priced games are scored (Q-07).
  • Do not attach a reward to ROI alone (SG-01).
  • Do not use a third-party depth chart to fill the injury gap (SG-04).
  • Do not adopt a blend weight fitted on 15 games (own-lines blend analysis).
