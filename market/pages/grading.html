The honest summary of Week 9

Fifteen games, three markets, one flat stake of 1 unit each, 45 settled bets. The result is 32–13 for +20.9% on turnover and +9.42 units. It is also p = 0.104 against the break-even rate implied by the prices actually taken, so it is not evidence of skill. Both statements are true at once, and the site prints both.

How the verdicts are produced

Three gates, in order

  • Break-even at the price taken. The break-even rate is recomputed from the prices actually available (58.2% across the book), not assumed to be 52.4%.
  • Sample size. Below 30 settled bets in a market, no ROI is called interpretable at all. The string UNDERPOWERED is attached to the row rather than left to the reader.
  • Exact binomial test against break-even. The p-value is two-sided and exact, not a normal approximation.

Reading the two number columns: for a side both are home handicaps, so a more negative number means we made the home team stronger than the market did; for a total both are points; for a moneyline both are probabilities on the side taken, and the market figure has had the book margin removed multiplicatively from the two published prices. Our probability minus the market's is the per-bet skill whose average the strategy board reports. Spreads and totals are priced at −110 because nfl.com publishes the number but not the price; that assumption is stated on the row and is the reason S1–S5 are not ranked.
