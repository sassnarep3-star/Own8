The distinction that matters

A correlation between outcomes is not a parlay price

Every value above is measured on realised outcomes. A parlay needs a joint probability for two legs that have not happened yet, conditional on everything known at kickoff. Those are different objects. The three surviving values say something real: a quarterback's passing yards travel with his own team's scoring (r = +0.385, n = 146) and with the game total, and the two opposing quarterbacks in the same game are positively associated (r = +0.542, n = 67). The practical consequence is the opposite of what a naive parlay builder assumes: treating those legs as independent understates the joint probability of two overs.

The smallest sample on the page

Nine weeks gives nine observations for the weekly-dependence question (C7). A confidence interval that wide is reported rather than hidden, and the absence of a significant weekly effect is not evidence that games in a week are independent. Treating unknown dependence as independence is the error this page exists to avoid.
